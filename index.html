Computational materials theory

Modelling excitons, light–matter interactions and optoelectronic materials.

I develop microscopic theory and computational approaches for the electronic, excitonic and optical properties of emerging semiconductors. My work connects materials structure, many-body interactions, phonons and quantum-state properties to experimentally measurable spectra, transport and device-relevant optoelectronic behaviour.

Research

Microscopic theory for optoelectronic materials.

My research combines many-body theory, model Hamiltonians and first-principles electronic-structure calculations to understand and predict the behaviour of low-dimensional, molecular and hybrid semiconductors. A recurring aim is to connect microscopic mechanisms directly to measurable optical and transport signatures.

01

Excitons & optical response

Modelling exciton formation, fine structure, selection rules, absorption, photoluminescence and nonlinear optical response in semiconducting materials.

02

Low-dimensional & hybrid materials

Studying TMDs, organic semiconductors, layered perovskites and van der Waals heterostructures, including interlayer coupling, moiré effects and hybrid excitons.

03

Phonons, dynamics & transport

Connecting electron–phonon and exciton–phonon interactions to relaxation, linewidths, diffusion, transport and temperature-dependent optical properties.

04

Quantum Phenomena

Using topology and quantum geometry where they provide useful microscopic insight into excitonic states, transport and optoelectronic response.
