Condensed-matter & materials theory

Excitons, quantum geometry and the physics of light in materials.

I develop theory and computational approaches to understand how the geometry of quantum states shapes interacting electron–hole systems, with a particular focus on exciton transport, topology and optoelectronic response.

Research

From hidden geometry to measurable physics.

The central question behind my work is how the internal structure of an exciton changes its motion, interaction with light and response to its environment — and how those effects can be isolated experimentally.

01

Exciton quantum geometry

Developing descriptions of exciton metric, Berry connection and Berry curvature across centre-of-mass and internal momentum spaces.

02

Transport & topology

Understanding how geometric and topological structure reshapes exciton motion, transport and response to external fields.

03

Optical signatures

Connecting quantum geometry to spectra, oscillator strengths, selection rules, nonlinear optical response and light-induced currents.

04

Materials modelling

Combining model Hamiltonians, many-body theory and first-principles calculations in low-dimensional and molecular quantum materials.
