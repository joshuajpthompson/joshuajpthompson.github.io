Excitons & optical response

From the formation of excitons to the light they absorb and emit, I study the microscopic mechanisms that shape optical properties in semiconductors.

Research focus

Light interacting with a semiconductor can create an exciton: a bound electron–hole pair whose behaviour depends on the electronic structure, screening and symmetry of the material. I develop microscopic theory to calculate these states and relate them to experimentally measurable quantities such as absorption, photoluminescence and optical polarization.

My work examines exciton fine structure, bright and dark states, exchange interactions and the optical selection rules that govern light emission. In transition-metal dichalcogenides and layered perovskites, I study how these properties change with the material structure, environment, magnetic field and dimensionality.

A recurring aim is to connect this fundamental description to function: controlling emission energy and polarization, identifying routes to localized single-photon sources, and understanding optical responses relevant to next-generation light-emitting and optoelectronic materials. I also investigate nonlinear and chiral optical effects, where symmetry plays a central role.
