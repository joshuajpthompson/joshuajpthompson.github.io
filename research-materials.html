Low-dimensional & hybrid materials

I investigate how composition, dimensionality and interfaces create new electronic and excitonic behaviour in atomically thin and hybrid semiconductors.

Research focus

Reducing a material to a few atomic layers changes how electrons interact, how excitons are bound and how light couples to the system. I study transition-metal dichalcogenides, organic semiconductors, van der Waals heterostructures and layered halide perovskites using electronic-structure calculations and excitonic models.

At interfaces between different materials, new processes become possible. My work on organic–TMD heterostructures has considered energy transfer, hybrid optical signatures and interlayer excitons, while studies of semiconductor heterobilayers address where excitons localize and how they interact. These systems offer ways to control excitations through stacking, alignment and choice of constituent materials.

I also study moiré structures, where a twist or lattice mismatch produces a much longer spatial pattern. Understanding how this pattern reshapes electronic and excitonic states is useful both for interpreting optical experiments and for investigating new approaches to light–matter coupling and material design.
