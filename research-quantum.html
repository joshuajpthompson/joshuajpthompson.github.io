Quantum geometry & topology

I explore how the quantum geometry and topology of electronic and excitonic states can influence optical properties and energy transport.

Research focus

The properties of a quantum state are not determined by its energy alone. Its wavefunction also carries geometric information, which can influence responses to external fields and the way excitations move. A central part of my research is to understand the quantum geometry and topology of excitons, which are composite electron–hole excitations rather than single particles.

I use theoretical models and microscopic exciton calculations to identify when this underlying geometry changes observable behaviour. In particular, I have investigated excitonic topology and quantum geometry in organic semiconductors, and shown how topological characteristics can enhance exciton transport. This highlights mechanisms that conventional descriptions based only on band dispersion may overlook.

More recent work examines geometric effects near band-gap closings, quantum-vortex excitons and the connection between many-body electronic states and optical response. My wider aim is to understand whether these quantum properties can provide useful design principles for optoelectronic materials and devices.
