Phonons, dynamics & transport

Excitations evolve after light is absorbed. I model how phonons, scattering and material structure control their relaxation, motion and emission.

Research focus

An exciton created by light does not remain in its initial state. It can scatter with lattice vibrations, move through a material, relax into lower-energy states or recombine to emit a photon. I calculate the microscopic interactions responsible for these processes and use them to model exciton populations, dynamics and spatial transport.

Much of this work is concerned with exciton–phonon coupling: how the phonon spectrum and the symmetry of a material determine which scattering pathways are available. In layered perovskites, I have investigated how a phonon bottleneck influences exciton emission. In atomically thin semiconductors, I have studied anisotropic diffusion, ultrafast transport and how momentum-dependent excitonic states affect these observations.

I am particularly interested in connecting first-principles calculations and many-body models to experimental spectroscopy and time-resolved measurements. Beyond understanding relaxation itself, this provides insight into how energy can be transported or retained before recombination—an important consideration for optoelectronic devices.
